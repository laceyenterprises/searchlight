> [!summary] Quick Peek
> - Purpose: Which search provider best finds verified new evidence on the four big grounding lines, and what that evidence says about them.
> - Result, on the pre-registered measure (verified new tier-1 claims: figures that enter a line's arithmetic), as corrected by Amendment 12:
>   - **Exa found the most across both arms and in the research arm, within the noise.** Both arms: Exa 16, Parallel 14, Tavily 13, Firecrawl 9. Research arm: Exa 11, Parallel 10, Firecrawl 5, Tavily 4.
>   - **In the search arm Exa tied Tavily at 11** (Parallel 8, Firecrawl 7), and ranks first only on the pre-registered tie-break, cost per claim.
>   - **Parallel's research agent was the most accurate at the same price:** 0.6% of its checkable items unsupported or false, against Exa's 4.6%, both at $0.10 a run.
>   - **Tavily's and Firecrawl's research agents trailed**, with 4 and 5.
>   - No lead is statistically significant: the tests are post hoc, and none survives a correction for the six comparisons.
> - As judged, before the corrections, the counts were larger and Exa's lead wider (research 30 / 19 / 12 / 6; search 26 / 15 / 16 / 12). The corrections count `_scratch/` facts as known in every question, move supply shares and the two unused denominators (g3, m3) to tier 2, count each fact once, and score Tavily's four cap-refused runs as returning nothing, as pre-registered.
> - Found by no other provider: Exa 6, Parallel 4, Tavily 4, Firecrawl 0. This rests on the judges' support lists, so a missed match would inflate it.
> - The evidence: no provider found a disclosed grounding volume or search-tool revenue for any of the four vendors. Those facets stay undisclosed. What did change for the sizing:
>   - ChatGPT's search runs mostly not on Bing, per third-party traffic studies; Microsoft says ChatGPT uses its supply for some answers.
>   - OpenAI said ChatGPT ran over 1 billion web searches a week (April 2025), which supports the supply side's ChatGPT volume.
>   - Anthropic's run-rate is about twice the $30B the sizing's line uses; doubling the line strains its own cross-check.
>   - Two of the KB's Microsoft terms facts are out of date.
> - Read this when: You're choosing a provider for evidence hunts like this one, or updating the sizing's big-vendor lines.
> - Skip this when: You need a public benchmark. This is internal and small-sample, and Parallel's terms restrict sharing benchmark results.
> - Key links: [Pre-registration](preregistration.md) (Amendments 8–12) · [Judge brief C](judge-brief-c.md) · [Experiment overview](overview.md) · Whole-market sizing (KB page `projects/exa-market-sizing/market-sizing`, not published)
> - Confidence: 0.55. Pre-registered, blind, and verified on the page. Lowered for n = 24 questions, one run each, agent judges, tier-1 finds concentrated in a few questions, and the post-hoc corrections of Amendment 12.

**Reading it.**
- **Exa leads the pre-registered measure across both arms and in research, by one or two claims.** In the search arm
  it ties Tavily and ranks first on cost per claim, the pre-registered tie-break. It also leads on all new claims in
  both arms and on useful tier-1 claims in research (8, against Parallel's 6).
- **Parallel is the most accurate research agent**, at the same $0.10 a run as Exa. In the search arm Parallel and Exa
  cost about the same per new claim ($0.0016 and $0.0017): a tie.
- **On claims no other arm found**, the search arms split: Parallel 3, Tavily 3, Exa 2, Firecrawl 0. In research Exa
  has 2, and Parallel and Tavily 1 each.
- **Cost depends on the credit plan.**
  - Per new tier-1 claim, Exa's research arm is cheapest at list prices ($0.22, against Parallel's $0.24).
  - At Firecrawl's Scale price, Firecrawl's research arm matches that ($0.22). It's also the cheapest per new claim of
    any tier in both arms ($0.0015 in search, $0.013 in research).
  - At its top-up price, Firecrawl's research arm costs $1.44 per tier-1 claim. Tavily's costs $0.93–$1.49.
- **Tavily's research agent** had the highest error rate (18.1% on the runs the primary counts). It also ran fastest,
  at 31 seconds.
- **Firecrawl's research agent** hit its 100-credit budget on 5 of the 24 questions and returned nothing on them.
- **The search arms cost cents**, and each found at least as many new tier-1 claims as its own research arm except
  Parallel's.

## How the corrections change the count (Amendment 12)
> [!cite] Section provenance
> - Source basis: `data/stage-c/corrections.json` (one entry per corrected claim, with its reason and evidence) and `scores_corrected.json`; the PR #38 review's findings M1–M3 and the owner's rulings [observed — 2026-09-27].
> - Authorship estimate: AI-computed; each correction is a rule applied to recorded data, not a new judgment of a page.
> - Confidence: 0.65

No provider was re-run and no claim re-judged. The corrections apply rules to the recorded verdicts:
- **Novelty.** Facts in `_scratch/` count as known, in every question. Four tier-1 claims the judges scored new are in
  the KB's red-team notes: OpenAI's launch prices (`o4#6`, `o1#10`), its June 2025 price cut (`o4#7`) and its deep
  research model prices (`o4#8`). Four tier-2 claims are corrected too, including Google's 16B tokens a minute
  (`g1#3`), which the g2 and g3 judges had found in the KB. The screens and their limits are in `corrections.json`.
- **Tiers.** ChatGPT's supply shares (`o5#1`–`#4`) are supply, tier 2 (prereg:315). g3 and m3 aren't "the
  denominator the sizing uses" (prereg:313): their own set entries say they're not used by the sizing. Their claims
  move to tier 2, apart from two known prices.
- **Duplicates.** A fact written in two questions counts once per provider-arm: the four pairs the review named
  (Meridian48, the 36.6% of prompts, Searchlab and FutureSearch), and a documented pass over every URL the questions
  share, plus two screens for a statistic relayed by a second page. The pass found 40 facts written in two or more
  questions (82 claims; 17 of the facts are new in the primary) and 7 claims that only bundle facts written
  separately.
  Anthropic's 2028 projection is one fact in a2 and a3; it takes a3's tier 2, since a 2028 figure doesn't enter the
  2026 line.
- **Tavily's refused runs.** "A run the caps refuse is logged as skipped and scored as returning nothing" (prereg:330).
  Amendment 10 set that rule aside and re-ran o3, m2, m3 and m6; the primary follows the pre-registration, so those
  four runs' items are out.

- **The direction survives every variant:** Exa is first or tied first in both arms, and first across both arms.
- **The size of the lead doesn't.** It is one or two claims in the corrected primary, and the search arm is a tie.
- **Two corrections go beyond the review's list:**

## Is the lead real? (post hoc)
> [!cite] Section provenance
> - Source basis: per-question counts of new tier-1 facts by arm from `scores_corrected.json`; exact two-sided sign tests, Holm's adjustment over the six comparisons, and question-level bootstraps (20,000 resamples, seed 7) in `score_c.py`. The tests, the bootstraps and the leader-against-each comparisons were chosen after the results were known; Amendment 12 logs them as post hoc [observed — 2026-09-27].
> - Authorship estimate: AI-computed.
> - Confidence: 0.6

As judged, the smallest of the six was Exa against Firecrawl in the research arm: p = 0.021 unadjusted, 0.129 after Holm's adjustment.
<!-- c:tests:end -->

- **In the corrected primary none of the six comparisons is significant, even unadjusted:** the smallest p is 0.219.
  They are six tests picked once the leader was known, so each p-value is also shown Holm-adjusted for six.
- **Against Parallel,** Exa is ahead on a few more questions in each arm, but one more run could reorder them.
- **Against Tavily's and Firecrawl's research agents,** the bootstrap intervals only reach zero (0 to 15 and 0 to 13)
  and the adjusted sign tests are far from significant. As judged, the unadjusted p-values were 0.039 and 0.021;
  adjusted, the smallest was 0.129, so the page's earlier claim of significance doesn't hold.

## What the evidence says about the four lines
> [!cite] Section provenance
> - Source basis: the judges' verified claims (`data/stage-c/claims.json`), each re-read on its page by a judge and, for the ones below, by the PR #38 reviewer. Third-party studies and estimates are labelled as such [observed — 2026-09-27].
> - Authorship estimate: AI-synthesized from judged claims.
> - Confidence: 0.6

**None of the four vendors discloses a grounding volume or a search-tool revenue.** Every question on those facets
ended unanswered or partial. The four lines stay imputed. What the providers did find:

**OpenAI** (the sizing's $60.0M line, plus the OpenAI–Bing flow on the partnership line):
- **Volume.** OpenAI's ChatGPT search product lead told WIRED that ChatGPT users were running more than 1 billion web
  searches a week, about 143M a day [extracted — 2026-09-27](https://www.wired.com/story/openai-adds-shopping-to-chatgpt/)
  (2025-04-28). It's the only volume figure from OpenAI that the judges found, and it covers ChatGPT, not the API.
- **Supply: mostly not Bing, per third-party traffic studies.**
  - Two studies relayed by Search Engine Land find ChatGPT's results come mostly from OpenAI's own index ("labrador")
    and from Google results scraped through Bright Data and Oxylabs:
    - in Chris Green's repeated-prompt test, the own index was the primary source 88.1% of the time
      [extracted — 2026-09-27](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-citations-change-hidden-search-pipelines-481843);
    - in Resoneo's replay, paid Thinking mode drew 75.3% of its results from scraped Google, and Bing didn't supply the
      own index [extracted — 2026-09-27](https://searchengineland.com/chatgpt-retrieval-stack-index-cache-pages-485036).
      That piece is a disclosed contributor article by Resoneo's co-founder.
  - Microsoft says ChatGPT uses its supply. Its FY26 Q4 call says Web IQ is used by many popular AI assistants,
    "including ChatGPT" [extracted — 2026-09-27](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4).
    Its search chief, Jordi Ribas, said ChatGPT uses Web IQ for some of its web answers (relayed by SEO Handbook)
    [extracted — 2026-09-27](https://seohandbook.co.uk/seo-news-updates/microsoft-web-iq-ai-agents-grounding-api/).
  - OpenAI's own help pages point to Microsoft too. The Enterprise and Edu page names Bing
    [extracted — 2026-09-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10093903-chatgpt-search-for-enterprise-and-edu), and
    the consumer search page points to Microsoft's privacy statement for its search providers
    [extracted — 2026-09-27](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt).
  - Other third-party readings are mixed:

    - What's new is third-party share figures, and they measure shares of results, not payments.
- **Denominator.** Third-party estimates of OpenAI's API revenue run from about $3B
  [extracted — 2026-09-27](https://www.thrad.ai/content/chatgpt-revenue-streams-breakdown) to about $12B

  The sizing's $4B / $6B / $8B sits inside that spread [inferred]. OpenAI discloses none.

## How it was run
> [!cite] Section provenance
> - Source basis: [Amendments 8–12](preregistration.md), [judge brief C](judge-brief-c.md), and the harnesses in `code/stage_c/` [observed — 2026-09-27].
> - Authorship estimate: AI-summarized from the pre-registration and code.
> - Confidence: 0.8

**The set.** 24 questions: four vendors times six facets (volume, revenue, denominator, price, supply, adoption).
Each question carried a KB context note.

**Two arms per provider.**
- **Search:** Stage A's adapters, unchanged, one call per question. The inputs differ by provider, as in Stage A:
  - Exa and Tavily got the question;
  - Parallel got the question plus three hand-written queries;
  - Firecrawl got only a hand-written keyword query.

  Stage A found that queries like these can leak answer terms (its D2 diagnostic). The review found no terms beyond
  the question text in `SV.json`.
- **Research:** each provider's research agent at its standard tier, with one shared prompt and output schema:
  - Exa Agent, effort medium, $0.10;
  - Parallel Task, processor pro, $0.10;
  - Tavily Research, model mini;
  - Firecrawl `/agent`, maxCredits 100.

  Tavily refused the shared schema, so it got the same fields in its own form (Amendment 9). That form drops the
  nested `required` lists, so Tavily was the only provider without required figure fields.

**Judging.**
- Each question's pool held every search result (deduplicated by URL) and every research figure and fact, shuffled
  with a recorded seed and stripped of provider names. That came to 771 result URLs and 1,370 research items.
- Eight judges verified each claim on its page, against a KB checkout at 2a055dc. Each graded three questions
  (`data/stage-c/grading/assignment.json`).
- Strict credit: a search result counts only if its own page states the claim, and a research item only if its cited
  URL does.
- Attribution was joined back only after every judge had finished. Blinding relied on instructions, though: the
  attribution maps sat in `grading/C/_attrib/`, inside the folder the judges read from. Stage B's write-up said to move
  them out of the judges' tree, and Stage C didn't.

**Time.** Latency is shaped by the harness:
- The research arms poll every 12 s (Exa, Tavily), 15 s (Parallel) or 35 s (Firecrawl), so their times are quantized.
  Firecrawl's runs at "72 s" took somewhere from 36 s to 72 s.
- Firecrawl's search time counts only the search call, not the three page scrapes that produce its text.

## Caveats
> [!cite] Section provenance
> - Source basis: this page, the pre-registration (Amendments 8–12), `corrections.json`, and the PR #38 review.
> - Authorship estimate: AI-written.
> - Confidence: 0.8

- **Small sample.** 24 questions and one run each, graded by Claude agents. The finds are concentrated, so the
  ranking between Exa, Parallel and Tavily isn't settled.
- **Post-hoc corrections.** Amendment 12's corrections, tests and cost ranges were made after the results and the
  review. Each correction is a rule applied to recorded verdicts and is listed with its reason, and the as-judged
  figures are kept beside them.
- **Novelty.** The primary counts facts in the KB checkout at 2a055dc as known, `_scratch/` included, in every
  question. Every new tier-1 claim was checked against the baseline, but tier-2 claims without a distinctive figure
  weren't re-checked against `_scratch/`. Six questions' judges (g5, g6, a4, a6, o4, o6) cite no `_scratch/` file, so
  "all new claims" may still count a few `_scratch/` facts. Stage B's rule, where `_scratch/` doesn't count, is shown
  as a sensitivity.
- **Duplicates.** The near-duplicate pass merges claims that state the same thing and drops bundles that add nothing.
  A claim that restates part of another question's claim and adds a figure or term is kept, so partly overlapping
  claims still count separately. The Web IQ terms in m4 and m5 are the main case.
- **Per-run budgets differ.** The research tiers were set by price class: Exa and Parallel at $0.10 a run, Tavily
  metered at about 37 credits a run ($0.19–$0.30 across plans), and Firecrawl capped at 100 credits. Firecrawl's cap is
  $0.075–$0.50 a run across plans; at Scale it's the smallest budget of the four. A higher cap would likely have
  avoided its five failures.
- **"Found by no other provider" depends on the judges' support lists.** With about 270 items per judge, a missed
  cross-provider match would inflate a provider's unique count. The judged files are committed, so the support lists
  can now be audited.
- **"Found" is page-level.** A research item is credited with any claim its cited page states, even one it didn't
  report. One credit is lenient: `o3#3` was credited through a Wayback copy of an earlier version of the page the items
  cite.
- **Blinding relied on instructions**, as above.
- **Costs.** Parallel's dollars are list-price estimates. Firecrawl's failed runs reported 0 credits; the export keeps
  only what it reported.
- **Internal only.** Parallel's customer terms restrict sharing benchmark results without its consent, so none of this
  may be published.

## What's stored
> [!cite] Section provenance
> - Source basis: `code/stage_c/`, `data/stage-c/` and `code/export_data.py` [observed — 2026-09-27].
> - Authorship estimate: AI-written.

- **`code/`:** `sets/SV.json` (identical to `stage_c/sets/SV.json`, which the code reads; both 00ca7b0449d6b38a), and
  `stage_c/` with the harnesses, configs, pooling, scoring and tables.
- **`data/stage-c/`:**
  - `claims.json`: 642 judged claims, in the judges' own words, with URL, verdict, tier, usefulness and the arms that
    stated each one, as judged. Social-media post URLs are hashed;
  - `corrections.json`: Amendment 12's correction layer, one entry per corrected claim with its reason and evidence,
    the screens behind it and the tier-1 baseline check;
  - `scores.json` (as judged) and `scores_corrected.json` (the corrected primary, its sensitivities and the post-hoc
    tests);
  - `cid_verdicts.json`: every research item's verdict;
  - `grading/`: the judges' files (ids, verdicts, tiers, support lists and notes, with any quote over 15 words cut),
    the attribution maps from item ids to provider-arms, and the judge-to-question assignment;
  - `runs_meta.json`: status, time, latency and cost per run, with no vendor text. `ts_utc` is UTC; the search
    harness logged local time (PDT), which the push log confirms.
- **Kept local and not committed:** the vendor responses and the pools, whose excerpts are vendor output.
- **Reproduce** from the committed record, with no API keys:

  ```bash
  cd experiments/search-provider-eval/code/stage_c
  python3 score_c.py --data ../../data/stage-c                              # rebuilds claims, scores and corrected scores
  python3 make_tables_c.py --data ../../data/stage-c ../../results-stage-c.md  # regenerates every table on this page
  ```
